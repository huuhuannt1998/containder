\title{CONTAINDER: Bounding Credential-Compromise Impact in
IEEE~2030.5 DER Systems}

\author{Huan Bui\corref{cor1}}
\ead{hbui11@charlotte.edu}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\affiliation{organization={University of North Carolina at Charlotte},
             city={Charlotte}, state={NC}, country={USA}}

\begin{abstract}
IEEE~2030.5 authenticates distributed energy resources with device certificates that do not
expire and cannot be revoked; the standard's sanctioned substitute is a local allow/deny list. We
show by measurement that denying a compromised identity reduces integrated feeder harm by exactly
zero. It refuses new sessions, while the adversary's established session keeps issuing commands
and its scheduled control keeps acting. We present CONTAINDER, which separates bootstrap identity
from short-lived attested operational authority and enforces expiry at the credential, session and
command-effect layers, deployable through a gateway proxy. Across two public feeders, twenty
paired seeds and $2520$ authorization arms on a corrected OpenDSS harness we find that adding
session termination to identity denial removes $48.8$--$54.4\%$ of integrated harm and adding
command cancellation $70.6$--$76.3\%$; that credential expiry alone removes none; and that command
cleanup alone also removes none, because the adversary simply re-issues over its still-open
session. Only both mechanisms together bound the residual effect at the cleanup latency. On the
authorization side, a reactive bound admitting zero absorption fails to contain where an
absorption floor of identical width contains, at negligible cost to volt-var service. The
consequence of withdrawing support, however, scales with the absorption the feeder is actually
drawing rather than with PV penetration, and at a compliant operating point it is an order of
magnitude smaller than at an overstressed one. Bounded lifetime truncates exposure rather than
attenuating it, at a measured cost in regulator operations. All cryptographic timings are
software-path; no hardware-backed key measurement was obtained.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Data availability}
The analysis engine, the X.509/mutual-TLS credential service, the OpenDSS feeder experiment
scripts, the pre-registration document, and every result file underlying the numbers reported here
are provided as a single archive accompanying this submission, and will be deposited under a
persistent identifier on acceptance; the archive is cited as
reference~\cite{dataset_containder}. Each reported numeral is checked against that archive by
\texttt{check\_numbers.py}, which fails if a numeral in the manuscript body, its abstract, the
Highlights or the supplementary material cannot be located in the released result files. The IEEE~8500-node and IEEE~123-bus test feeders are publicly
available from the referenced OpenDSS distributions. The one input we cannot redistribute is the
IEEE~2030.5-2023 standard text itself, which is sold by IEEE; Section~\ref{sec:threat} states
precisely which claims depend on it and which edition each was checked against.
