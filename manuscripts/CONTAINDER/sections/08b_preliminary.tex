\section{Pilot Evaluation}\label{sec:prelim}
% provenance: jrn_01KXTX3PH43VR78AM1GZHC27T1 (real in-session results: credsvc + OpenDSS).
% All numbers here are measured, at reduced scale; the full pre-registered sweep remains future work.

This pilot exercises the full pipeline end to end and produces measured results at deliberately
limited scale. The cyber-side engine, credential service, OpenDSS feeder pipeline and
availability fault injection are implemented and measured on a workstation, using the real
IEEE~8500-node feeder~\cite{arritt2010ieee8500}; native EPRI-client traffic, constrained-hardware
attestation, a GridLAB-D cross-check, a HELICS federation, interoperability tests and the full
pre-registered sweep are not. These results establish that the mechanism and the measurement work but do not replace the
confirmatory sweep; the limitations paragraph closing this section states what each reaches.

Throughout, the primary physical endpoint is the \emph{induced overvoltage area}
$\Delta J_V$: the attack-case value of the one-sided overvoltage term of $J_V$, summed over all
energized nodes as $\sum_i \max(0, V_i - 1.05)$, minus its base-case value at the same load
state. Positive values mean the attack pushed nodes above the ANSI~C84.1 upper
band~\cite{ansi2020c841}. A single power-flow solution yields $\Delta J_V$ in per-unit nodes
(p.u.-node); the time-series experiment integrates the same quantity over a one-minute step, in
per-unit node-minutes (p.u.-node-min). Each table and figure is labelled with the unit it carries.


\paragraph{Functional invariants and containment (C1, C2).}
The credential service uses real X.509 / ECDSA certificates and an EAT-style attestation token.
Ten implemented security invariants all pass on the prototype: bootstrap identity cannot authorize
control,
issuance requires a valid fresh attestation, the certificate is bound to a freshly generated
operational key, replayed evidence is rejected, an expired credential cannot issue commands,
per-command scope matches issuance, a bad measurement yields only a narrowed safe-mode scope, a
prior epoch cannot self-renew, and a copied prior operational key is contained at the next epoch.
With per-command revalidation the post-expiry acceptance gap is
$\Delta_{\mathrm{expiry}} = -0.5$\,s (the last accepted adversarial command precedes expiry),
against $+5.0$\,s without enforcement, where it grows to the cached session age. A fresh
operational key is generated each epoch, so a copied key is useless after rotation.
Table~\ref{tab:invariants} lists the invariants, the identifier each carries in the released
result file, and their outcomes. These are the invariants the prototype implements and tests;
they are not a proof of the design, and properties outside this set are untested.

\paragraph{Overhead (C3).}
On the workstation tier, over 500 iterations, median software-path latencies are $0.03$\,ms for
key generation, $0.06$\,ms for attestation construction, $0.23$\,ms for a full attested issuance
and $2.3$\,ms for a real mutual-TLS handshake, and the issuer sustains about $4900$ renewals per
second single-threaded. These are standard EC~P-256 primitives dominated by one ECDSA signature
and verification, so constrained-controller cost can be estimated from public-key throughput.
They are software-path timings, not deployment latencies, and no hardware-backed measurement
exists.

\paragraph{Availability (C6).}
The renewal safety margin $T_{\mathrm{TTL}} - (T_{\mathrm{renew,p99}} + T_{\mathrm{net}})$ stays
positive at every tested lifetime down to five minutes and a $100{,}000$-device fleet re-mints in
about $20$\,s, so short lifetimes are latency-feasible. The risk is instead a verifier outage
longer than the lifetime. Injecting verifier outages, fail-closed denial retains $0.77$ of
legitimate control availability, a bounded grace period $0.83$, and degraded-scope safe mode
$1.0$. The injection is in-process; networked outages, clock skew, and renewal storms remain.

\paragraph{Time-series containment: what the lifetime mechanism does, and what its ratio is not (C5).}
% provenance: experiments/run_timeseries2.py -> results/timeseries2.json (n=20 paired seeds, 2 states).
A compromised credential drives the DER from minute five of a sixty-minute horizon on the real
IEEE~8500-node feeder, and its lifetime determines whether the malicious setpoint keeps reaching
the feeder. Every (state, seed, policy) arm is an \emph{independent} compile with a fresh PV fleet
and fresh regulator and capacitor state, at twenty paired seeds and two operating states. That
isolation matters: the predecessor compiled once per seed and iterated policies against the same
live circuit, so each arm warm-started from the previous arm's excursion, and the integrals an
earlier version reported ($6956$, $7030$, $3196$, $3.8$) inherit that leak. \textbf{We withdraw
all four.} Non-convergence is retried at most twice, tripling the control-iteration budget each
time ($500 \to 1500 \to 4500$); $24$ solves were flagged and $48$ retried, and unsettled solves are
retained and flagged, never dropped. \textbf{Every flagged solve falls in a lifetime-bounded arm
of a single seed} (light load, seed $1019$: nine steps in B3 and A4, six in B5, none in any
long-lived arm), so we report recovery below both with and without it. Results below are at the conformant Category~B envelope of $5.28$\,kvar --- the envelope
an operator must authorize to receive volt-var at all (Table~\ref{tab:scope}).

\emph{Scope alone does not contain.} A long-lived credential confined to that envelope holds the
feeder in violation for the entire horizon in $20/20$ seeds, median integral $5656$ p.u.-node-min
(bootstrap CI $[5356, 5857]$, light load).

\emph{The lifetime mechanism truncates rather than attenuates, and recovery is prompt.} While the
setpoint is applied the instantaneous excess area holds at a mean of $101.8$ p.u.-node (SD
$10.4$), and the paired per-minute accrual under the CONTAINDER and legacy arms is
\textbf{bit-identical over the window they share} (maximum absolute difference $0.0$, all twenty
seeds, both envelopes). Lifetime enforcement changes nothing about how fast harm accrues, only
when it stops. What it does change could have come out otherwise, \emph{under light load}: at expiry the feeder
returns to its pre-attack baseline within one minute in $17/20$ seeds, the settled post-expiry
excess has median $0.000$ p.u.-node, and the whole post-expiry contribution is $1.74\%$ of the
integral. Nothing latches --- a regulator or capacitor left in an excursion-driven position could
have held voltage up after the credential died, which would have made the mechanism useless.
Excluding seed $1019$, the one seed carrying every flagged solve, the figure is $16/19$ --- that
seed is not what carries the result.

\textbf{Under normal load it does not hold, and we report that rather than the light-load figure
alone.} The same twenty seeds, the same arms and the same criterion give recovery in only $2/20$
seeds, a settled post-expiry excess of median $0.193$ p.u.-node, and a post-expiry tail of
$5.57\%$. We believe the criterion, not the mechanism, is what fails there: recovery is declared
when the excess falls below a fixed $0.05$ p.u.-node for three consecutive minutes, and the
normal-load pre-attack baseline is itself only $0.152$ p.u.-node, so the threshold sits inside the
feeder's own baseline variation and is not resolvable. That is a defect in our endpoint, and we
do not read the normal-load numbers as evidence that cleanup fails; we read them as evidence that
this experiment can only speak to recovery at light load. A relative recovery criterion is
what the confirmatory sweep should use.

\emph{The resulting ``$2.16\times$ reduction'' is therefore not an effect size, and we do not
report it as one.} With the accrual rate identical across arms and the residual negligible, the
integral is that rate times the minutes the credential is honoured, so the ratio is pinned to the
ratio of exposure windows, $(60-5)/25 = 2.20$, net of the recovery tail. The data behave exactly
so: the mean ratio is $2.159$ (CI $[2.131, 2.183]$) at the conformant envelope and $2.159$ (CI
$[2.128, 2.184]$) at the narrower $3.6$\,kvar one --- \emph{the same number to four significant
figures at two operating points whose accrual rates differ by $43.7\%$} --- and no seed in any
state or envelope exceeds $2.1988$. A physical mechanism does not return the same ratio at two
different physical operating points; arithmetic does. The defensible statement is the relation
behind it: integrated harm is a measured accrual rate times the credential lifetime, up to the
exposure window, so the containment factor is $(\text{exposure window})/T_{\mathrm{cred}}$ --- a
quantity the operator chooses, not one we measured. Quoting $2.16$ would be quoting our own choice
of a twenty-five-minute lifetime against a sixty-minute horizon back to the reader.

\emph{The honest counter-arm, and the cost.} At the non-conformant $0.6$\,kvar envelope the
ordering reverses: CONTAINDER is \emph{worse} than legacy (median $20.1$ against $12.4$; ratio
$0.622$, CI $[0.455, 0.781]$; $0/20$ seeds favour it, and $0/20$ at ratio $0.083$ under normal
load). Where the envelope is already inert the lifecycle buys nothing and the transition at expiry
costs something: tap operations rise from a median of $78.5$ to $125$, a $59.2\%$ increase. The
same cost appears at the conformant envelope, $67$ to $101.5$, a $51.5\%$ rise in regulator wear
the voltage endpoint does not price. Lifetime enforcement pays only where scope cannot contain,
which is precisely the conformant case --- but it is not free.

\emph{Two arms withdrawn as uninformative.} The ablations A2 (session enforcement removed) and A3
(command cleanup removed) are \textbf{not identified by this harness}: its activity predicate maps
the \texttt{legacy}, \texttt{no\_session} and \texttt{no\_cleanup} lifecycles onto one branch, so
A2, A3 and B1 are a single arm under three labels, their twenty-seed output series byte-identical
in both states; A4 and B3 likewise. The earlier claim that removing session enforcement or command
cleanup ``returns the feeder to no recovery, so each mechanism is necessary'' was reading a
relabelling as a result, and we withdraw it. The per-command enforcement those ablations were
meant to probe \emph{is} tested, but on the cyber side --- invariants I5, I6 and I10 and the
$\Delta_{\mathrm{expiry}} = -0.5$\,s measurement above.

\emph{A label correction.} The arm this artifact calls ``full scope'' commands $(12$\,kW,
$3.6$\,kvar$)$ while the arm it calls ``narrow ACL at IEEE~1547'' commands $(12$\,kW,
$5.28$\,kvar$)$, and the inverter's own reactive limit is $5.28$\,kvar. The ``narrow'' envelope is
thus a strict \emph{superset} of the ``full'' one, which is why the legacy arm reads $5656$ at the
conformant envelope against $3851$ at the narrower one. That is not narrowing making things worse;
it is an inverted label, and no comparison in this paper reads a scope \emph{ordering} off those
two arms. Table~\ref{tab:scope}, which sweeps the envelope continuously and names each level by
its commanded kvar, is the evidence for scope.

\paragraph{Feeder consequence across operating states (C5).}
We run an export/overvoltage attack on the real IEEE~8500-node feeder (4876 buses, 8531 nodes,
1177 loads, $10.8$\,MW aggregate load at nominal multiplier) with 600 seeded rooftop-PV units of
$12$\,kW each ($7.2$\,MW), ten paired seeds, under three load states.
Because the 8500 base has native undervoltage at its extremities, we report the induced
\emph{overvoltage} area the attack targets rather than a two-sided area that would credit the
attack for lifting sagging nodes; this attack-specific endpoint was chosen after observing the base
undervoltage, while the two-sided area remains the declared primary system-level endpoint.
Throughout, $\Delta J_V$ is measured against \emph{legitimate scheduled PV operation under an
IEEE~1547-2018 Category~B volt-var curve}, not against a feeder with no DER.

\paragraph{How narrow must scope be before it stops containing? (C5)}
% provenance: experiments/run_scope_envelope.py -> results/scope_envelope.json
Fixing two envelopes by fiat cannot answer this, so we sweep the authorized envelope
continuously along the two axes an IEEE~2030.5 \texttt{FunctionSetAssignments} narrowing can act
on, at five paired seeds and two operating states (Table~\ref{tab:scope}).

\textbf{First, the state these numbers are measured in.} The light-load condition places
$7.2$\,MW of PV against $3.24$\,MW of load: \textbf{$222\%$ penetration}, a deliberately extreme
hosting condition. At that penetration the \emph{legitimate} feeder, unattacked and under the
conformant Category~B curve, already carries a median overvoltage area of $57.7$ p.u.-node (range
$56.5$--$65.7$); with the PV commanded to zero the same feeder reads $1.7$. The base case is
therefore itself well outside the ANSI band, and every $\Delta J_V$ below is an increment on
that. Two consequences we state rather than let a reader infer. A ``return to baseline'' in this
paper means a return to that non-compliant baseline, not to compliance. And the reactive result
below is measured where the volt-var service is already failing to hold the band on its own,
which is a hosting-capacity condition as much as an authorization one. Under normal load
($67\%$ penetration) the legitimate base area is $0.36$ and no envelope trips.

On the \emph{active-power} axis, where the adversary holds \texttt{DERControl}, there is a genuine
threshold: under light load $\Delta J_V$ stays negative up to $\sigma{=}0.7$ of nameplate and turns
positive between $\sigma{=}0.7$ and $\sigma{=}0.8$, with the IEEE~1547 trip screen firing in
$60\%$ of seeds at $\sigma{=}0.7$ and in every seed from $\sigma{=}0.8$. Restricting active-power
authority below roughly $70\%$ of nameplate does contain.

On the \emph{reactive} axis it does not, and this is the paper's most consequential result.
Confining the adversary to the volt-var function set --- the narrowest scope that still delivers
the service IEEE~1547-2018 requires and CSIP volt-var programs procure --- \textbf{breaches at
every envelope we tested, including an envelope of zero}. At the full Category~B capability of
$5.28$\,kvar ($44\%$ of nameplate) the median induced area is $87.9$ (per-seed range
$79.9$--$100.8$, all five seeds tripping OV1 at $\ge 1.10$\,p.u., $v_{\max}$ up to $1.142$). At
$0$\,kvar it is still $31.2$, with every seed tripping. The mechanism at zero is worth stating
plainly: an adversary authorized only to configure volt-var need not inject anything: it is enough
to \emph{suppress} the reactive absorption the feeder was relying on, which is squarely inside a
volt-var-only authorization. \textbf{There is therefore no reactive envelope that both contains a
compromise and delivers the volt-var service.}

This overturns an earlier version, which placed ``narrow'' at $0.6$\,kvar --- $11.4\%$ of the
Category~B range --- and reported that it held the feeder inside the ANSI band. That envelope does
not deliver volt-var, so the containment it appeared to show was the containment of authorizing
almost nothing; measured against legitimate operation rather than an empty feeder, even it
breaches ($\Delta J_V = 17.4$, trips in $4/5$ seeds). We withdraw the claim that narrow scope
``retains the most common legitimate DER function while bounding the attack'': at the tested
envelope it did not retain the function, and at the conformant envelope it does not bound the
attack.

Consequence is strongly state dependent. Under normal load the same sweep gives a median $\Delta
J_V \le 12.0$ on the active axis and $\le 11.4$ on the reactive axis, with no trips at any envelope
in any seed. Light load with high PV is the binding state, as Section~\ref{sec:formalism}
anticipates.

\paragraph{Why $\Delta J_V$ is negative at small envelopes.}
The negative entries on the active axis are neither noise nor reactive ``support''. Against a
counterfactual in which PV generates at its full scheduled output, an adversary held below
$\sigma{=}0.7$ is \emph{curtailing} relative to that schedule, so feeder voltage rise falls and the
one-sided overvoltage area shrinks. A restrictive active-power scope therefore converts the
compromise into an unwanted-curtailment problem --- an availability and revenue harm our voltage
endpoint does not price --- rather than an overvoltage one. An earlier version attributed such
values to a narrowed reactive envelope ``marginally supporting'' voltage, which is backwards:
support raises voltage and would \emph{increase} the area. The mechanisms are curtailment
relative to schedule on the active axis and reactive \emph{absorption} on the reactive axis.

\paragraph{This attack is loud, and that matters.}
At the conformant reactive envelope under light load, a median $6958$ of $8531$ energized nodes
($82\%$; per-seed range $6489$--$7139$) sit above $1.05$\,p.u., with a median maximum voltage of
$1.138$\,p.u.\ rising to $1.142$\,p.u.\ --- figures taken from the corrected harness, not from the
superseded one. The qualification the previous paragraph requires applies here too: at $222\%$
penetration a large share of those nodes is already above band before any attack, so what the
attack adds is the $87.9$ p.u.-node increment, not the whole excursion. What is nonetheless true
is that the post-attack state is not a stealthy manipulation but a feeder-wide excursion that AMI voltage reporting,
regulator operation counts, and customer complaints would surface within a single metering
interval. Two conclusions follow. First, it weakens any narrative in which such a compromise
quietly persists for a certificate's two-year lifetime: the operative containment latency becomes
the utility's detection-and-response time, not the credential lifetime. Second, it identifies the
attacker our evaluation does \emph{not} cover --- one who holds voltages just inside the ANSI band,
or acts only during peak-PV minutes, would gain far more from long persistence precisely because
nothing alarms, and that is exactly the case in which lifetime rather than detection binds. Our pre-registered protocol specifies an operationally realistic telemetry-limited
attacker alongside the oracle worst case; the stealth-constrained variant is the more important of
the two for this paper's thesis, and it is not yet run. We regard this as the most significant gap
in the present evaluation.

\paragraph{Results measured under the superseded scope configuration.}
Three sweeps predate the scope-envelope result and used the earlier $0.6$\,kvar ``narrow''
envelope and a zero-DER counterfactual. \textbf{Their legacy-baseline halves stand.} Under a
legacy full-authorization compromise, induced overvoltage grows with PV penetration from $8$ at
$20\%$ to $254$ at $100\%$ (five seeds, Fig.~\ref{fig:penetration}); it is reachable through
active or reactive authority alike, at $122$ (export-limit), $97$ (volt-var) and $57$ (volt-watt)
(five seeds, Fig.~\ref{fig:families}); and it appears on a second public feeder, the IEEE~123-bus
system, at up to $2.2$ under high penetration (three seeds), that stiffer regulated $4.16$\,kV
feeder needing more penetration to breach the band. What does \emph{not} stand is the
accompanying claim that the narrowed scope held induced overvoltage near zero in every cell and
therefore ``retains the most common legitimate DER function while bounding the attack''. That
envelope is $11.4\%$ of the Category~B reactive range, does not deliver volt-var, and its
near-zero readings were taken against a feeder carrying no DER; Table~\ref{tab:scope} supersedes
them. We have not re-run these three sweeps at the conformant envelope, so the conformant-scope
result rests on one penetration level, one feeder, and the two operating states of
Table~\ref{tab:scope}.

\paragraph{Modeled retained authority, and what it is \emph{not} evidence for (H2).}
% provenance: jrn_01KXWJ4TPTK2DGSBKY5YCF50T5 (real 8500 full baseline + ablation sweep).
An earlier version summarised the baselines with a proxy $E_{\mathrm{phys}} = \Delta J_V \times
\BRauth$ and reported that moving from ephemeral to legacy persistence ``multiplies light-load
exposure roughly $1700\times$''. \textbf{We withdraw that figure.} Both cells share an
authorization scope and therefore the same measured $\Delta J_V$ of $126.60$, so it cancels and
the ratio reduces to $17524/10 = 1752.4$ --- a quotient of two \emph{assumed} lifetimes,
containing no measurement. We also rename the quantity: $E_{\mathrm{phys}}$ is a
consequence-times-duration product in p.u.-node-hours, not the flexibility-time $\BRexp$ of
Section~\ref{sec:formalism}, which carries kW$\cdot$h and kVAr$\cdot$h. The paragraph belongs to
hypothesis H2; an earlier version labelled it H1, which the pre-registration reserves for the
lifetime-by-scope interaction.

Two entries in Table~\ref{tab:exposure} contradict the narrative that version drew from it.
First, \textbf{B5's retained authority ($12.22$\,h) exceeds B3's ($10.0$\,h)}: on that dimension
the full design is worse than ephemeral-only. B3's credential expires and is never renewed,
whereas B5's is attestation-gated and \emph{does} renew, surviving a $\mathrm{Geometric}(p)$
number of cycles, so at $T{=}6$\,h and $p{=}0.5$ the expectation is $T/p = 12$\,h. Renewal is what
makes CONTAINDER deployable --- an un-renewed credential strands the legitimate device too --- and
its price is that containment latency is set by detection probability, not by lifetime. Second,
\textbf{B2 and B5 both sit at $\approx 0$ here, which we no longer read as support for the
design}: that is the arithmetic of a ``narrow'' scope whose measured $\Delta J_V$ was exactly
$0.0$, and any lifetime times zero is zero. \textbf{We therefore withdraw the sentence ``neither
half alone suffices'' as argued from this table.} What stands in its place is measured and
narrower: at the conformant envelope, scope alone does not contain (the legacy arm never recovers
in $20/20$ seeds), so duration is the only remaining control --- with the magnitude of the benefit
being the lifetime the operator sets, as established above.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
These results are pilot results and we do not generalize them. Physical results come from two
public feeders and a single OpenDSS solver, with no GridLAB-D cross-check and a fixed
export attacker; Table~\ref{tab:seeds} gives the full seed inventory.

\emph{The IEEE~9500 failure mode, stated.} The PNNL~9500-node system was obtained but did not
converge in our build, and we name the mode rather than leave it an unexplained absence. The
failure is in the \emph{base case}, with no attack and no PV of ours placed: the model's own
shipped driver, unaltered, returns \texttt{Converged = False} with an empty error description and
every bus per-unit magnitude reading zero --- the solution collapses rather than missing
tolerance. It reproduces at $1000$ iterations, under both the normal and Newton algorithms, and
with feeder controls enabled and disabled, so it is neither a control-loop oscillation nor an
iteration-budget shortfall. Our build is DSS C-API $0.14.5$ / DSS-Python $0.15.7$ /
OpenDSSDirect.py $0.9.4$ on macOS arm64. \textbf{We did not resolve it and we do not attribute
it}: we cannot exclude a build defect on our side, having had no second OpenDSS build to
discriminate. The consequence is that cross-feeder generality here rests on the IEEE~123 run
alone, which is weak, and we claim no more.

Retained-authority figures and the exposure proxy built on them are model outputs under assumed
attestation and lifetime parameters, not measurements. Overhead is workstation-only, with no
constrained-hardware or TEE measurement. The availability result injects verifier outages
in-process rather than as networked or hardware failures; clock skew and renewal storms are
untested. Incremental deployment is by construction, interoperability against an unmodified
IEEE~2030.5 endpoint is untested, and the clause-level delta between the 2018 and 2023 editions
remains open pending access to the revised standard. Most importantly, what this evaluation
supports is narrow: at the one conformant envelope and two operating states tested, scope does
not contain, credential expiry ends the excursion promptly, and the size of that benefit is the
lifetime an operator sets. It does not establish an effect size for CONTAINDER, and the
pre-registered sweep --- two feeders, a cross-solver check, seasonal states, constrained
hardware, a stealth-constrained attacker, and the full seed budget --- is what would.
