\section{Feeder Simulation Methodology}\label{sec:feeder}
% provenance: power/confirmatory.py, power/metrics.py, power/profiles.py.
% The superseded pilot harness (power/feeder8500v2.py) is retained in the artifact but supplies

Physical consequence is measured by translating an authorized-control set into a dispatch program
and running it on public distribution feeders. Every confirmatory result uses one harness,
\texttt{power/confirmatory.py}, on two feeders: the IEEE~8500-node feeder~\cite{arritt2010ieee8500}
and the IEEE~123-bus feeder, both built from \texttt{PVSystem} objects with an inverter rating and
an \texttt{InvControl} volt-var controller on the IEEE~1547-2018 Category~B default
characteristic~\cite{ieee1547-2018}. The counterfactual throughout is that same fleet at its
\emph{legitimate scheduled output} under that characteristic, not a feeder with no DER. The PNNL
9500-node system~\cite{anderson2022ninethousand} did not converge in our build, in its base case
under its own shipped driver, and contributes no number; Section~S1 of the supplement names the
mode and the build versions so the absence is an explained negative result.

\paragraph{Two corrections that the reported results depend on.}
The predecessor harness contains two defects, both found by re-measuring its own arms, and both
invalidate the comparison its central claim rested on.

\emph{The two reactive primitives were not magnitude-matched.} The curve arm writes its setpoint
into an \texttt{XYCurve} read by an \texttt{InvControl} declared
\texttt{RefReactivePower=VARMAX}, so its axis is per unit of \texttt{kvarmax}; the fixed-setpoint
arm multiplies by the apparent-power rating. At the nominally identical label $q_{\mathrm{high}}
= -0.44$ the two commanded $-2.15$ and $-5.28$\,kvar per DER, a factor of $2.46$. Any row-wise
comparison of the two therefore compared two different physical absorptions --- exactly the
mismatched-feasible-set confound that makes a control primitive look causal when only the bound
differs.

\emph{The legitimate counterfactual under-delivered volt-var.} The conformant Category~B
characteristic absorbs $44\%$ of \emph{nameplate} at $V_4 = 1.08$\,p.u., which against a
\texttt{VARMAX} reference of that same quantity is $-1.0$; the predecessor wrote $-0.44$ and
absorbed less than half. The baseline against which every induced quantity was differenced was a
degraded service. Correcting it lowers the light-load legitimate overvoltage area from $57.1$ to
$46.1$ p.u.-node.

Both are fixed by carrying every reactive quantity in \textbf{physical kvar} and converting to
whichever per-unit reference each OpenDSS object consumes at a single site. A third change is
methodological rather than a defect: penetration is swept through \emph{per-unit rating} rather
than fleet count, because IEEE~123 has $91$ load buses and a fleet-count sweep saturates above
that, returning an identical circuit at every larger fleet.

\paragraph{Endpoints.}
The primary endpoint is the two-sided ANSI~C84.1 Range~A violation area, $J_{\mathrm{band}}$,
summing overvoltage and undervoltage excursions over all energized nodes. The one-sided
overvoltage term is retained as an attack-specific secondary and reported alongside it, never
instead: a one-sided endpoint credits an attack for lifting sagging nodes, cannot price the
undervoltage that a forced absorption floor causes, and --- as \S\ref{sec:results} shows --- scores
a stealth attacker that curtails the entire fleet as an \emph{improvement}. Service quality is the
shortfall of realised reactive output against what the conformant characteristic prescribes at
each unit's observed terminal voltage, reported as an excess over the paired legitimate arm.

\paragraph{Threshold screen, not a trip.} Every node is screened against the IEEE~1547-2018
Category~II overvoltage thresholds (OV2 $1.20$\,p.u./$0.16$\,s; OV1 $1.10$\,p.u./$2.0$\,s), plus a
substation reverse-power flag. We call the outcome a \emph{screen} throughout and never a trip,
because the model has no persistence timer, no disconnection and no post-disconnection state
update: a crossing means a DER at that node would be required to cease to energize, not that a
trip was simulated.

\paragraph{Control mode.} We solve with \texttt{controlmode=static}, which does \emph{not} disable
the feeder's controls, and release the diagnostic that shows it
(\texttt{results/controlmode.json}). Reaching the legitimate base case at light load moves $8$ of
$12$ regulators for $32$ tap steps and changes $6$ capacitor states. Controls are inert only under
\texttt{controlmode=off}. Regulators and capacitors therefore act against the disturbance in every
result reported, and tap operations are reported throughout, including where CONTAINDER increases
them.

\paragraph{Horizon experiments.} The lifecycle experiments step a moving exogenous state:
irradiance on a midday arc with a cloud transient placed deliberately inside the post-expiry
window, and load rising toward evening. Within an arm the circuit is stepped forward without
recompiling, so taps and capacitor states carry over and post-expiry recovery is a measurement
rather than a construction; arms remain independent of one another, each its own compile. Under
the predecessor's static profile, integrated harm was exactly (accrual rate)~$\times$~(minutes
honoured), so no lifetime comparison could report anything a multiplication would not.
