\section{Impact Accounting, and Why Reachability Alone Cannot Rank These Designs}\label{sec:formalism}

This section fixes notation and says why we need it. As the introduction states, it is \emph{not} a
contribution: an extended attack
graph~\cite{poolsappasit2012dynamic,wang2008attackgraph,frigault2008measuring} yields exactly the
accounting below and so agrees with us rather than competing. We keep the notation only because the
experiments in Section~\ref{sec:results} move ``how much'', ``for how long'', and ``under which grid
conditions'' differently, and one scalar cannot express that.

\paragraph{Substrate and the four quantities.}
A static privilege graph $G=(V,E)$ has principals and resources as nodes and access relations as
edges; $x \subseteq V$ is a set of compromised credentials, and a policy $\pi=(L,\mathrm{Att},A)$
gives a lifetime function $L$, an attestation-gated renewal predicate $\mathrm{Att}$, and a scope
map $A$. The device and grid state $u_t$ and the feeder power-flow and protection model $F$ appear
nowhere in $G$, which is the point.

\emph{Cyber reach} $\BRreach=\Reach(x,\pi)$, a count, is the least fixed point of the
$\pi$-enabled access relation from $x$: the endpoints the compromised authority can send an
authenticated request to. \emph{Commandable flexibility} $\BRflex$, in kW and kVAr, is the
aggregate over reachable DERs of the feasible set-point region
$\mathcal{C}_d(t;u_t,A_\pi(d))$ given irradiance, state of charge, and inverter limits; a
read-only scope yields the current set-point alone, hence zero. \emph{Retained authority}
separates three durations, in seconds,
\begin{equation}
\BRauth=\max\big(T_{\mathrm{cred}},T_{\mathrm{sess}},T_{\mathrm{cmd}}\big),
\end{equation}
where $T_{\mathrm{cred}}$ runs until the stolen credential is refused for a \emph{new} session,
$T_{\mathrm{sess}}$ until already-authenticated sessions can no longer issue commands, and
$T_{\mathrm{cmd}}$ until queued or latched controls stop affecting the DER; the capacity-time
exposure $\BRexp=\int \BRflex(t)\,\mathbf{1}[\text{authority effective}]\,dt$ carries
kW$\cdot$h or kVAr$\cdot$h. This split is the design's load-bearing one: expiry bounds
$T_{\mathrm{cred}}$ only. \emph{Physical consequence} $\BRphys=\max_{a}J_V(F,u,a)$ is the worst
outcome reachable within the adversary's authority, where $J_V$ is the voltage-violation area
outside the ANSI~C84.1 band weighted by magnitude. $\BRphys$ carries two units and we label
every table with which: \emph{p.u.-node} evaluated at one operating point, and
\emph{p.u.-node-min} accumulated over a horizon. Secondary metrics are maximum per-unit
deviation, violating-node count, protection trips, regulator tap operations, energy not served,
unnecessary curtailment, and recovery time.

\paragraph{Three separations, true by construction.}
Call a measurement \emph{reachability-only} if it is a function $M$ of $\Reach(x)$ alone. Then, at
fixed reachable set, varying the scope map changes $\BRflex$; an attestation-gated ephemeral
credential and a long-lived unrevoked one differ in $\BRauth$ and $\BRexp$; and the same
authorized command is harmless at one operating state and trips the IEEE~1547 overvoltage screen
at another. \textbf{None of these can fail}, since $M$ is single-valued in $\Reach(x)$ and each
construction varies a quantity that is by definition not an argument of $M$. They restate a
definition. We keep them only because they say which quantities an evaluation must move, and each
ships as a regression test in the released engine. \textbf{They are not evidence for the design;
that is in Section~\ref{sec:results}, and only there.}
